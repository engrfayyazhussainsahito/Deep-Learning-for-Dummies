About & contributing
Open course · MS level · 18 weeks

Learn deep learning
the way it is actually done.

An open, graduate-level course on deep learning, from perceptrons to diffusion models. Every week pairs rigorous derivations with runnable PyTorch, original diagrams and interactive experiments you can poke at in your browser. Free, open source and built to be studied from end to end.

All weeks

Jump to any chapter. Your progress is saved in this browser.

01

Introduction, Mathematical Foundations, and Tooling

What deep learning is and where it came from, a self-contained refresher of the linear algebra, calculus, probability and information theory you will use all semester, and a working PyTorch toolkit.

02

Neural Networks and Backpropagation

From the perceptron to multilayer networks, why they can approximate almost anything, the activation and loss functions that shape training, and a complete derivation of backpropagation and automatic differentiation, with a from-scratch NumPy lab.

03

Optimization for Deep Learning

Why training deep networks is a hard non-convex problem and how the practical toolbox (SGD, momentum, Adam/AdamW, learning-rate schedules, careful initialization, clipping and normalization layers) makes it work.

04

Regularization, Generalization, and Training Practice

Why a model that fits the training set can still fail on new data, and the toolbox for fixing it (weight decay, dropout, label smoothing, early stopping, mixup and CutMix), plus the engineering habits (hyperparameter search, debugging, logging, checkpointing, honest evaluation) that make deep learning experiments trustworthy.

05

Convolutional Neural Networks

Why locality, weight sharing and equivariance make convolutions the right prior for images; the arithmetic of convolution layers (stride, padding, dilation, 1x1, depthwise-separable, pooling, receptive fields); the gradients of a conv layer; LeNet, AlexNet and VGG; and how to train an image classifier on CIFAR-10.

06

Modern Vision Architectures, Transfer Learning, Detection and Segmentation

Residual learning and why skip connections make very deep networks trainable; ResNeXt, DenseNet, Inception, MobileNet, EfficientNet and ConvNeXt; transfer learning and fine-tuning; object detection (R-CNN family, SSD, YOLO, anchors, IoU, NMS, mAP); semantic and instance segmentation (FCN, U-Net, DeepLab, Mask R-CNN).

07

Sequence Models: RNNs, LSTMs, and the Road to Attention

How to model data that arrives in order. Recurrent networks, backpropagation through time and why gradients vanish or explode, LSTM and GRU gating, sequence-to-sequence learning with teacher forcing and beam search, and the attention mechanism that made recurrence optional.

08

Representation Learning, Embeddings, and Midterm

How to turn symbols, users and items into useful vectors. Distributed representations, word2vec (skip-gram, CBOW, negative sampling), GloVe and fastText, embedding geometry and bias, subword tokenization (BPE, WordPiece, Unigram), metric learning with contrastive, triplet and InfoNCE losses, plus a complete review of Weeks 1 to 7 and a practice midterm.

09

The Transformer Architecture

Self-attention from first principles, multi-head attention with exact tensor shapes, feed-forward layers, residuals and normalization, positional encodings from sinusoids to RoPE and ALiBi, encoder/decoder variants and causal masking, the quadratic cost and its cures, parameter and FLOP counting, training tricks, and a from-scratch GPT in PyTorch.

10

Large Language Models and Pretrained NLP

Pretraining objectives (BERT, GPT, T5), scaling laws and Chinchilla compute-optimal training, emergent abilities and their critiques, parameter-efficient fine-tuning (adapters, LoRA, QLoRA, prefix and prompt tuning), instruction tuning, in-context learning and chain-of-thought, alignment with SFT, RLHF and DPO, retrieval-augmented generation, evaluation, hallucination, decoding strategies, a Hugging Face lab, and a guide to reading and presenting papers.

11

Vision Transformers, Multimodal Models, and Self-Supervised Learning

ViT, DeiT and Swin with exact shapes and the CNN-versus-ViT inductive-bias debate; DETR and SAM; self-supervised learning from pretext tasks to SimCLR, MoCo, BYOL, DINO and MAE (with an InfoNCE derivation); CLIP and zero-shot transfer; captioning, VQA and multimodal LLMs; and a lab that fine-tunes a ViT, runs CLIP retrieval and linear-probes self-supervised features.

12

Generative Models I: Autoencoders, VAEs, and GANs

A taxonomy of deep generative models, then autoencoders (undercomplete, denoising, sparse), variational autoencoders with a full ELBO derivation, the reparameterization trick, beta-VAE, posterior collapse and VQ-VAE, and GANs from the minimax game and optimal discriminator to WGAN-GP, DCGAN, conditional GANs, CycleGAN, StyleGAN and evaluation with IS, FID and precision/recall. The lab builds a VAE and a DCGAN on MNIST.

13

Generative Models II: Diffusion, Flows, and Autoregressive Models

Denoising diffusion from first principles (forward process, reverse process, the variational bound and the epsilon-prediction loss), noise schedules and v-prediction, score matching and the SDE/probability-flow view, DDIM and fast samplers, classifier and classifier-free guidance, latent diffusion with U-Net and DiT backbones, normalizing flows and autoregressive models, a comparison of all generative families, and the societal risks of synthetic media. The lab trains a small class-conditional DDPM on MNIST and explores sampling steps and guidance.

14

Graph Neural Networks and Geometric Deep Learning

Learning on graphs and other structured domains. Graph data and tasks, adjacency, degree and Laplacian matrices, the message-passing framework, GCN (with a derivation of normalized propagation and its spectral motivation), GraphSAGE, GAT and GIN, expressive power and the Weisfeiler-Lehman test, over-smoothing and over-squashing with their remedies, pooling and readout, node, link and graph tasks, graph Transformers, and the geometric deep learning blueprint (symmetry, invariance, equivariance) with point clouds, PointNet and equivariant networks. The lab uses PyTorch Geometric on Cora and a molecular dataset.

15

Deep Reinforcement Learning

Learning to act from reward. MDPs, returns, value functions and the Bellman equations (derived), dynamic programming, Monte Carlo and temporal-difference learning (Q-learning, SARSA), function approximation and the deadly triad, DQN with replay and target networks, Double, Dueling and prioritized variants, the policy gradient theorem (derived), REINFORCE with baselines, actor-critic, GAE (derived), A2C/A3C, TRPO and PPO, continuous control (DDPG, TD3, SAC), exploration, model-based RL and world models, offline RL, and the connection to language models (RLHF, reward models, PPO, DPO, GRPO). The lab trains DQN on CartPole in PyTorch and PPO with Stable-Baselines3.

16

Efficient and Scalable Deep Learning

Making deep learning fit, run fast and ship. GPU memory hierarchy, arithmetic intensity and the roofline model, profiling and bottleneck analysis, mixed precision (FP16 vs BF16, loss scaling), gradient accumulation and checkpointing (the sqrt(n) trade-off derived), data/tensor/pipeline parallelism, ZeRO and FSDP memory accounting, FlashAttention (online softmax derived), KV caching and speculative decoding, pruning, quantization (PTQ, QAT, int8/int4, GPTQ/AWQ), distillation, low-rank factorization, Mixture-of-Experts, NAS, and deployment with ONNX, TorchScript, torch.compile and serving. The lab profiles a training loop and applies AMP, quantization and distillation while reporting accuracy and latency trade-offs.

17

Trustworthy Deep Learning and Theory

What it takes to trust a model, and what we do and do not understand about why deep learning works. Adversarial examples (FGSM and PGD derived, linearity explanation, threat models, adversarial training, certified defenses, transferability), distribution shift and OOD detection (max softmax, energy, Mahalanobis), uncertainty (aleatoric vs epistemic, ensembles, MC dropout, ECE, temperature scaling, conformal prediction), interpretability (saliency, SmoothGrad, Grad-CAM, Integrated Gradients, attention caveats, probing, mechanistic interpretability), fairness, privacy (membership inference, differential privacy, DP-SGD), safety and responsible release, theory highlights (randomization tests, implicit regularization, NTK, lottery tickets, grokking, double descent) and open problems. The lab attacks and defends a classifier, implements Grad-CAM, and measures calibration.

18

Final Projects, Course Review, and Final Exam

The capstone week. A guide to giving a 12-minute project talk and writing a conference-format report, with honest reporting of baselines, ablations, seeds, error bars and failure cases; a reproducibility checklist and grading rubric; a cumulative review of Weeks 1 to 17 with key equations, a decision guide and a debugging checklist; a 43-question practice final exam with solutions; and next steps (reading papers, reading lists, reproducing papers, open source, careers).

How to study with this course

  1. Read actively. Try each derivation on paper before opening the collapsed solution.
  2. Play with every demo. Change one thing at a time and predict the outcome before you look.
  3. Run the labs. Copy the code into Colab or a local environment and break it on purpose.
  4. Do the exercises. They are graded from warm-up to research-flavoured; the hard ones are where learning happens.
  5. Read the papers. Each week ends with essential reading marked. Skim, then read closely.

Prerequisites

Linear algebra, multivariate calculus, probability, Python/NumPy and an introductory machine learning course. Week 1 contains a compact math refresher.

Setup

Python 3.10+, PyTorch, and a free Google Colab account are enough for every lab.
